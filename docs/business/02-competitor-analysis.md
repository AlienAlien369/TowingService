## 2.1 How the industry is structured

Roadside assistance worldwide is sold in three ways, and they have very different economics:

1. **Membership clubs** (AA and RAC in the UK, ADAC in Germany, AAA in the US). Customers pay an annual
   subscription. Revenue is recurring and prepaid, so the club can afford its own patrol fleet. **Highly profitable.**
2. **B2B2C assistance providers** (Agero, Allianz Partners, Europ Assistance, TVS Auto Assist, Urgently). Insurers
   and car makers pay per vehicle or per job. The provider runs a control room and **subcontracts the physical work
   to local tow operators**. Margins are thin, and the business depends on a few big contracts.
3. **Lead marketplaces and local operators** (Justdial, Google listings, thousands of independent towers). Highly
   fragmented. The information layer (Justdial) is very profitable; the towers themselves are small cash businesses.

RoadSaathi starts as a consumer marketplace (model 3, done properly), sells its supply network into model 2, and adds
memberships (model 1) once the service is trusted.

## 2.5 What the numbers teach us

1. **Recurring revenue wins.** The two most profitable roadside businesses in the world (RAC 39 %, AA 30 %) earn
   annual memberships *before* any breakdown happens. → RoadSaathi adds an RSA membership in year 2 (chapter 7).
2. **Pure middlemen lose.** Urgently had a modern app and big OEM contracts but thin margins, customer
   concentration and debt. It shrank 10 % and was sold for about US$5.50 a share. → We must keep a real
   take rate, diversify clients, and avoid debt-funded growth.
3. **Information is cheap to sell, fulfilment is expensive.** Justdial earns 29 % EBITDA by selling leads; the
   towers do the hard work. → Our software (operator SaaS, white-label) should earn margin with no truck cost.
4. **Indian consumers pay for standardised trust.** Urban Company built a ₹1,144 cr business on upfront prices,
   trained partners and guarantees. It took about ten years, which shows how long patient capital takes. → Trip PIN,
   ratings, invoices and damage process are the product, not extras.
5. **Governance is existential.** GoMechanic's collapse came from accounting, not product. → Integer-paise ledger,
   immutable signed contracts, audit log (already built) plus audited books from year 1.
6. **B2B assistance companies are customers, not only rivals.** TVS Auto Assist, Allianz and Europ subcontract to
   local operators. A dense, tracked, verified Delhi network is something they would buy.

## 2.6 Our positioning statement

> For vehicle owners in Delhi-NCR who are stuck on the road, **RoadSaathi** is the towing and roadside service that
> shows the price before you book, tracks the truck live and only lets the verified driver start the job. Unlike
> calling numbers from a listing, every job comes with a trip PIN, a rating and a GST invoice. For insurers,
> dealers and fleets, RoadSaathi is a ready, tracked fulfilment network with an API.
